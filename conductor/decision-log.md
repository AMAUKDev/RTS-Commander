### DECISION-003: Map-driven AI variety, points layer first
- **Date**: 2026-09-13
- **Track**: strategic-points_20260913
- **Decision**: AI variety comes from geography (resource sites, villages, hilltops, bases), not personalities or deeper adaptation. Resource sites = existing industrial buildings + generated fill; mines only at sites; control points pay only while garrisoned by ≥ MinGarrison ground vehicles; per-base flat income. Delivered as four tracks: points (this), platoons with objectives, truck logistics, air/naval support tasking.
- **Rationale**: User observation: enemy AI mine-spams at its base and convoy-rushes one road. KISS: one new concept ("point") that every later system reads. Take-and-hold at platoon scale is the desired feel.
- **Alternatives**: Spacing rule only (no objectives to fight over); points as destroyable buildings (bombing targets, not held ground); personalities.
- **Impact**: New `Points` settings section, new strategic-points service, two AI decisions changed, COMMANDER LOG window, point markers on map and world.

### DECISION-005: Platoon operations doctrine
- **Date**: 2026-09-13
- **Track**: platoon-operations_20260913
- **Decision**: Missions-and-requisitions architecture (approach A). The mod claims every AI-bought vehicle at the depot; platoons of 6 (3 armour, 1 carrier, 2 AD) fill FOB, picket and attack missions; offensives run on 2–3 equidistant axes, sized to the commander's own tracked picture, with release points and a pressure clock (attack at least every 12 min). Buyer fills an order book of requisitions. AI-only this track.
- **Rationale**: Today's behaviour is a trickle to points plus one convoy at the nearest enemy. Explicit missions are legible in the COMMANDER LOG and give offensives a shape. Contact-weighted point value, axis-only manning gates and the pressure clock stop the sides expanding outward without meeting.
- **Alternatives**: Extend the garrison step and redirect game convoys (no scaling, still one stream); per-vehicle utility scoring (never forms up).
- **Impact**: New Operations/ service replacing the garrison partial and the home guard's recruit logic; buyer gains requisition mode; new markers and log block; new OPERATIONS settings.

### DECISION-006: Truck logistics shape
- **Date**: 2026-09-13
- **Track**: truck-logistics_20260913
- **Decision**: Mines and every control point pay only by truck; base income stays per tick. One persistent truck per paying point, round-trips to the nearest held base, pays on entering the ring, returns empty. A lost truck loses only its load and is replaced free on the next cadence. 3-minute loads.
- **Rationale**: User wants traffic both ways and a raidable economy without escort rules or a cost spiral. FOBs from the platoon track already sit on front-line points.
- **Alternatives**: Mines only (too little traffic); everything including base income (too much); truck consumed on arrival (one-way traffic); truck fee per spawn (can go net negative under pressure); escorts by rule (couples to platoons).
- **Impact**: New Logistics/ service, hopper accrual replacing direct payout for mines and control points, truck markers, IN TRANSIT readouts, Logistics settings section.

### DECISION-007: Air support tasking driven by the ground plan
- **Date**: 2026-09-13
- **Track**: air-support-tasking_20260913
- **Decision**: The operations mission list is the single source of air objectives — no separate air brain. CAS-only sorties over contested objectives first (platoon in contact or live attack), then front ForwardBase missions by threat; pickets and rear points get none. Magnitude scales with `CountObserved` + `ObservedFloors` (ladder 0→0, ≤2→1, ≤5→2, ≤9→3, ≥10→4 airframes; cap 4 CAS + 1 CAP escort per objective; wing ceiling 8 everywhere). CAP escort only when hostile air is tracked inside the objective ring (`ThreatMemorySeconds` freshness). The player-side AI tasks only airframes it bought itself; player AIR missions are never retasked. Launches ride the existing `BuyAirframe`/`TryLaunchAiAircraft` path on the existing `AirFund`; the duel-only gates on the air buy and task legs are removed so it runs on every commanded HQ, stock missions included. Losses re-request only while contested, behind a 2-min cooldown doubled to 4 when ≥2 hostile air-defence units are observed at the loss. Naval out of scope.
- **Rationale**: Today the enemy wing orbits the opponent's frozen opening airbase and only exists on the duel map. CAS bound to the platoon plan makes the air force fight over the same ground the platoons fight over, and the existing launch, recovery and budget machinery applies unchanged.
- **Alternatives**: A smarter fixed strike target in `TaskAirWing` (still a separate brain); commander launches through `TrySpawnFromRecipe` for recipe AUTO-relaunch (local-HQ + raw-funds path that bypasses the AirFund); scaling per platoon rather than per objective (more sorties than a wing of 8 can fly).
- **Impact**: New `Operations/CommanderOperationsAir.cs` partial on the operations service; a pending-launch claim notify on the existing `RegisterFactionUnit` postfix; `ChooseAirRole` reads sortie demand; duel gates deleted; 2 new config entries (Operations section); SelfCheck ladder cases; no new Harmony patches.

### DECISION-008: Picket insertion by transport helicopter rides the SAM supply machinery
- **Date**: 2026-09-13 (user-approved same day with four answers folded in)
- **Track**: heli-picket-insertion_20260913
- **Decision**: Rear picket points farther than 2 km from the nearest road (the user's gate, verbatim intent "more than 2km from road is fine") are filled by air instead of by road; a roadless map flies every picket. The operations service requests a transport heli, the two picket vehicles are bought as cargo mounts at heli spawn (charged their `cargo.value` each) — one air-defence plus the cheapest other mountable ground vehicle, per the user's "doctrine over bargains" answer — and the heli is rented (charged at spawn, refunded on recovery via the existing `PurchasedWithFunds` chain), flown to the point's hold-post ring by the existing `AIHeloTransportState` patches, landed and unloaded only (airdrop resolved out of scope), RTB'd and recovered. One insertion airborne per HQ, one request per review, 10-minute loss cooldown per point (confirmed); a lost heli loses its hull cost and the two vehicles aboard (engine behaviour: `MountedCargo.OnPartDetached` spawns cargo disabled). Funded from the ground pot, never the `AirFund`, `BuyAirframe` or `TaskAirWing` — the air-support wing (DECISION-007) and insertions buy and fly separate aircraft. Road distance is measured against the road polylines discovery already caches (`roadPointLists`, retained past discovery instead of cleared).
- **Rationale**: Pickets drive from the depot over the road network, so roadless rear points (hilltops, remote villages) pay nothing for most of the match and yield to platoons on the pool guard. The SAM supply feature already proved the whole buy-fly-land-unload-return chain in play; the only engine unknowns the capture ponytail flagged are asset data (which vehicles are mountable cargo), resolved by a once-per-mission roster log with drive-fill as the fallback.
- **Alternatives**: Gate on distance from the nearest held asset (design's first proposal, 10 km — the user replaced it with the road-distance gate: distance-to-road is what actually predicts a slow drive); physically loading pool vehicles aboard (no game API — loading is loadout at spawn, verified in the assembly); spawning the vehicles at the point when the heli arrives (teleports combat power, no delivery risk); sling loading (player-flown mechanic, no AI hook); airdrop variant (parachute-capable mounts exist but descent is unverified in play — user kept it out of scope).
- **Impact**: New `Operations/CommanderOperationsInsertion.cs` partial; road polylines retained past discovery (`Points/CommanderStrategicPointDiscovery.cs` — the `StepBases` clear removed) plus a `NearestRoadDistanceMeters` helper on the points service; an HQ-parameterised AI entry generalising `CommanderSupplyHeliService`'s local-HQ spawn gate (second caller after `RequestSamSiteFoundationDrop`); insertion branches in `HoldDeployedCargo`, the ejection suppression and `PruneFinishedMissions`; `PicketMembers` adoption of deployed vehicles; `heli=` on the `Ops … review:` line (the air-support track extends the same line with `air=[…]` — reconcile at implementation); 4 new Operations config entries, 1 new slider row, SelfCheck cases.

### DECISION-009: One spending ladder — strict home CAP, then a weighted draw
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: commander-priorities_20260914
- **Decision**: Every commanded HQ spends from one pot per review in a fixed ladder: (1) home CAP, strict — 2 fighters with active-radar-homing missiles over its airbases, plus one per two tracked enemy aircraft within 30 km (no upper limit), plus one per CAP fighter lost to enemy air in the last 10 minutes; nothing below is bought while CAP is short. (2) Platoons and their air support, (3) air-delivered pickets, (4) buildings share the remainder by a weighted draw each review (60/20/20) with a 10 % floor for any rung with open demand. The economy service's structure loop and the separate air fund stop spending on their own; both become rungs.
- **Rationale**: Four unconnected pots made priority emergent; on a map this size the platoon order book is never empty, so pickets and buildings were rarely reached, and nothing guaranteed a fighter screen over the commander's own base. The user asked for CAP first and "some variability between each decision loop", not between commanders.
- **Alternatives**: Retune the existing pot shares (cannot make CAP strict or guarantee lower rungs); serve one rung per review in rotation (lumpy; platoons wait two reviews in three); per-commander personalities (explicitly not wanted).
- **Impact**: `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderService.cs` review becomes the single spend site; `GetEnemyBuildReserve` no longer withholds; `AccrueFund`/`AirFund` retired; new settings `HomeCapBaseline`, `HomeCapPerEnemyAircraft`, `LadderPlatoonWeight`, `LadderPicketWeight`, `LadderBuildingWeight`, `LadderRungFloorPercent`; one `ladder:` log line per review.

### DECISION-010: Smarter air wing — ordnance, helicopter CAS, packages, AWACS, ARAD, idle sweep
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: smarter-air-wing_20260914
- **Decision**: CAS loadouts strongly prefer AGM-68 and AGM-48; attack helicopters are the CAS airframe for forward bases, pickets and platoons in contact while jets fly attacks and pre-emptive cover; sorties with more than one airframe form up at an orbit 12 km from base and go in together (bounded by a 3-minute wait); one AWACS per commander orbits 30 km behind the front; three or more tracked enemy air-defence vehicles within a 5 km cluster near an objective open an anti-radiation sortie ahead of that objective's CAS; every commander-owned airframe without a mission is retasked each review.
- **Rationale**: Sorties arrived one aircraft at a time because each airframe was sent the moment it launched; CAS carried whatever the generic scorer picked; no radar or anti-radiation employment existed; owned airframes could idle.
- **Alternatives**: Parallel multi-base launches (rejected by the user in favour of a form-up orbit); helicopter choice by distance to a friendly base (user chose by mission kind); a separate wing-planner service (duplicates the sortie table).
- **Impact**: New sortie kinds (Awacs, Arad, package state) in `Operations/CommanderOperationsAir.cs`; ordnance preference in the CAS scorer; settings `PackageFormUpSeconds`, `RotaryCasRangeMeters`, `AradClusterMinimum`.

### DECISION-011: Airframe selection by fitness tier
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: airframe-selection_20260914
- **Decision**: Airframes are ranked per role into tiers from the game's own anti-air / anti-surface ratings (Fighter, Multirole, Strike, LastResort; reversed for CAS). The highest tier a held base can launch always wins; within it the commander buys the best-rated airframe when the threat is high and funds allow, the cheapest when quiet; a tier is never dropped for price, only when nothing above it can launch. Applies to every commander airframe pick, including the choice among already-owned airframes and the idle sweep.
- **Rationale**: The same-day capability change let any airframe that could hang an air-to-air missile fly CAP on price, so Crickets and Compasses flew CAP with Revokers available, and fighters could be sent to CAS ahead of Brawlers.
- **Alternatives**: Hand-written per-airframe lists (brittle); a pure fitness/price score (what produced the problem).
- **Impact**: Tier table and selection rule beside the role buyer in `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAir.cs`; tier shown on `Air roster` and launch lines; constants `FighterRatio` 1.5, `CapThreatAircraft` 2, `CasThreatObserved` 3.

### DECISION-012: Ground tactics — wide ring, defence arc, bounding advance, counter-attack
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: ground-tactics_20260914
- **Decision**: A holding platoon spreads on the point's full hold radius (air defence inside), forms a defence arc toward the threat with tanks and IFVs forward when contact is detected, and returns to the ring 60 s after contact lapses. An attacking platoon leaves the road at its release point and advances in 800 m cross-country bounds in line, waiting for half the platoon to close each bound, and never paths a road within 1 km of a tracked enemy. A reinforcing platoon arriving at a point that is already held counter-attacks the tracked enemy from a flank 1 km off the threat bearing, or screens 800 m out on the most threatened approach when no enemy is tracked, folding into the ring only if the garrison drops below minimum.
- **Rationale**: Posts at 60 % of the radius bunched the platoon; reinforcements shared the same posts; attackers drove the road into a defended point in column because the contact drill fires only on a tracked hostile.
- **Alternatives**: Approach-covering ambush posts (user chose ring default + adaptive arc); reverse-slope posts (too much terrain sampling); a separate tactics service (duplicates the move seam).
- **Impact**: `EnsureHoldPosts` / `DriveToHoldPosts` in `Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs`, posture handling in the movement tick, bounding in `CommanderOperationsOffensive.cs`, new marker texts; settings `DefenceArcStandoffMeters` 400, `BoundMeters` 800, `OffRoadRangeMeters` 1000.

### DECISION-013: Pickets first — pickets capture, platoons only toward the enemy, book-only buying
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: pickets-first_20260914
- **Decision**: Two-vehicle pickets capture and hold every control point that is not a front point and take pool vehicles ahead of new platoon formation. A platoon forms only for a purpose facing the enemy — a forward base on a front point, an attack, or one standing reserve — and dissolves to the pool when its purpose resolves. Ground buying is order-book only once the operations service owns the force; the platoon cap is deleted. Up to three insertion flights airborne and three requests per review. Reinforcement platoons per request 6; home CAP max 4 and the 10-minute insertion cooldown unchanged.
- **Rationale**: Platoons were formed from whatever the pool held and the plan buyer kept filling the pool, so 27 platoons spread over a map with no enemy near most of them, while the 8-platoon cap never bound (any open requisition lifted it) and pickets starved behind platoon formation.
- **Alternatives**: Fixing the cap's "lifts while open" condition (keeps the spam, just smaller); a per-commander vehicle budget (indirect).
- **Impact**: `PlanPickets`/`FillPickets`/`PlatoonsNeedThePool`/`TryFormPlatoon` in `Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs`, `GroundBuyingCapped` → `GroundBuyingBookOnly` in `Operations/CommanderOperationsRequisitions.cs` and the buyer, `PlanInsertions` limits, `OperationsReservePlatoons` default 1, `OperationsHeliInsertionLimit` default 3, `MaxReinforcementPlatoons` 6.

### DECISION-015: FOB cap, spacing, abandonment, and the captured-depot handover
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: fob-construction_20260914
- **Decision**: A commander owns at most `FobMaxPerCommander` (2) forward operating bases, counted off
  the live base list so a captured one counts against its captor; sites must be `FobMinSpacingMeters`
  (20 km) from any other FOB as well as 15 km from any airbase; only the front-nearest held point is
  ever a candidate; the order opens only when the building rung has banked the full price; losing one
  stops all FOB building for `FobLossCooldownMinutes` (15). At the cap, a quiet and unused rear FOB at
  least `FobAdvanceMeters` (20 km) further from the enemy than the candidate point is demolished and
  its base torn down, at most once per 10 minutes, so the next order can go in at the front. On
  capture, a depot is re-registered with its new owner, and no faction's deployment loop will spend
  supply at a depot it does not own.
- **Rationale**: The user's "we don't want EVERY capturable point turning into a FOB" and "we should
  also be able to abandon a FOB and then build one closer to the front-line". The depot handover
  closes a game bug where the losing faction bought vehicles that spawned for the winner.
- **Alternatives**: Any front point as a candidate (rejected: a whole line of candidates); a Harmony
  hook on `Airbase.CaptureFaction` for the handover (rejected: the event fires before the buildings
  change hands and before a depot knocked out in the fight is repaired, so a one-shot re-registration
  is pruned and never retried).
- **Impact**: `Operations/CommanderOperationsFob.cs`, `Economy/CommanderFobBuilder.cs`,
  `Depot/CommanderFactionVehicleService.cs`, three settings, one new self-check entry point.

### DECISION-014: FOB construction by air or road; depots on captured airbases
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: fob-construction_20260914
- **Decision**: A commander may order one FOB at a time on a control point it holds that is at least 15 km from any airbase and nearest the front: three deliveries (transport helicopters carrying construction cargo, or three munitions trucks by road) converge, then the mod creates a real runtime airbase (400 m capture ring, capturable) and spawns a vehicle depot, a radar and a helipad inside its build ring. Trucks are consumed; helicopters return and are recovered. Fixed recipe. Any captured airbase lacking a depot gets one first from the building rung, then a helipad if it lacks a pad.
- **Rationale**: The game exposes a public runtime airbase creator and a depot registers itself with the faction, so a FOB becomes a genuine spawning base with no Harmony patch; captured bases were unused for spawning when they had no depot.
- **Alternatives**: Destroy-only FOBs (rejected: capturable is worth fighting over); vehicles kept as garrison (rejected for trucks); commander-chosen recipes (rejected for now); helicopter building cargo (does not exist).
- **Impact**: New `Operations/CommanderOperationsFob.cs` partial, supply-heli construction cargo branch, ladder building-rung wish order, settings `FobMinBaseDistanceMeters`, `FobEnabled`.

### DECISION-016: Nearest-first tasking; a forward base's truck follows its platoon
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: platoon-operations_20260913 (addendum), pickets-first_20260914
- **Decision**: (1) A forward base gets a munitions truck dispatched, and posts a truck buy, only once at least one of its platoons is Holding the point; a base whose platoons have all left returns its truck to the pool. The truck chosen is the pool truck nearest the point. (2) A road picket fills from the pool vehicle nearest its point, armour last, and never a munitions truck. (3) A forward-base or attack mission takes the nearest available platoon, not the first in list order.
- **Rationale**: User report: "individual trucks driving around, often straight into the enemy by themselves" and tasking that "seems random, rather than towards closest". Two sources of lone trucks were found: every planned forward base drew and bought a truck from the review it was planned (15 bought, most to `0/2` bases), and the picket fill took the pool's cheapest vehicle by value, which is the truck (value 2).
- **Alternatives**: Escorting the truck inside the platoon's formation (deferred: the platoon movement seam does not carry a non-member); keeping cheapest-first for pickets with trucks excluded (rejected: still ignored distance).
- **Impact**: `Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs` (`FillMissionTrucks`, `AssignPlatoons`, `FillOnePicket`, `ForwardBaseWantsTruck`, `PicketCandidateRank`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsRequisitions.cs` (truck line gate); five new self-checks. New log lines: `<base>: truck follows <platoon> to the forward base (n km)`, `<base>: no platoon holds the point; its truck returns to the pool`.


### DECISION-017: Air buying is money-limited; suppression flies first and flies armed
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: smarter-air-wing_20260914 (departure)
- **Decision**: (1) The wing's buy loop continues while its funds cover the cheapest airframe an
  OPEN demand wants and the airborne count is under `AirborneCeiling`; `MaxAirBuysPerReviewSafety`
  (12) is a runaway guard only. This **supersedes the decision of 2026-09-13 that set
  `MaxAirBuysPerReview` to 3**. (2) When the ground buyer is held to its order book, that book is
  empty and the wing has an open request, the ground's unspent share of rung 2 (less the naval ask)
  is accrued into the air fund, bounded by the fund's existing ceiling. (3) An objective whose ring
  shows `AirDefenceHesitationCount` (2) or more tracked air-defence vehicles gets jet CAS only until
  an anti-radiation sortie has been on station over the belt; the memory is dropped when the belt
  thins below the threshold. (4) An aircraft may be bound to a suppression sortie only when its LIVE
  weapon stations carry an anti-radiation store, and a commander-built suppression loadout takes a
  real anti-radiation missile (ARMSeeker: ARAD-116, ARAD-45) on every pylon that accepts one and
  fills the rest with AGM-99, then AGM-68, then any other ground store.
- **Rationale**: The 2026-09-14 match logged `air demand: CAP 0/13, CAS 1/33, ceiling 13/20,
  budget 214` with the balance climbing past 600 and only two to four launches a review, and
  `holds: balance 491, unit budget 152, 7 vehicle types on offer (5 quiet reviews)` beside it.
  Cheap SAH-46 Chicanes were fed into seven to twenty-three observed air-defence vehicles until
  every sortie read `cooldown`. And an `FS-12 Revoker` tasked with an anti-radiation strike had been
  bought through the FIGHTER role path, so it flew air-to-air missiles against the belt: the binding
  test asked what the TYPE could carry, not what the aeroplane was carrying.
- **Alternatives**: Raising the per-review cap from 3 to a larger number (rejected: the same
  arbitrary limit, differently placed); making the air fund unbounded rather than handing the
  ground's surplus over (rejected: the ceiling is what stops the wing hoarding from the ground);
  opening the anti-radiation sortie at the hesitation count rather than at `AradClusterMinimum`
  (rejected: two separate tuned thresholds, and the cluster rule is the one that decides whether a
  belt is worth a sortie at all); ranking AGM-99 and AGM-68 as the PRIMARY suppression ordnance as
  the user's wording suggested (rejected on the game's own data — `resources.assets` describes the
  AGM-99 as an anti-ship missile and the AGM-68 as an optically guided missile, and neither carries
  an `ARMSeeker`, so making either primary would have made every strike aircraft a suppression
  candidate and sent sorties against radars with nothing that homes on one; they are ranked first
  among the SECONDARY stores instead, which is where the user's intent and the asset data agree).
- **Impact**: `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAir.cs` (`MaxAirBuysPerReviewSafety`, the money-limited
  `AirBuyContinues`, `ReadAirDemandPrices`, `MayBindToRole`, `FillsAirRoleNow`, the launch note and
  `CheckAradOrdnancePreference`), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderLadder.cs` (`GroundShareGoesToWing`),
  `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderService.cs` (the hand-over in `SpendPlatoons`, `GroundShareToWing`),
  `Operations/CommanderOperationsAir.cs` (`RotaryCasAllowed`, `AradMemorySurvives`, `LastAirDefence`,
  `AradFlown`, the five binding sites, the review line),
  `AirCommand/CommanderAirCommandScoring.cs` and `AirCommand/CommanderAirCommandLoadout.cs`
  (`PreferredAradSecondaryOrdnance`, `ScoreAradMountForCommander`, `CarriesAntiRadiation`,
  `AutoConfigureAradLoadout`). Twenty-nine new self-check cases.

### DECISION-018: The wing banks for what is asked; the airborne ceiling scales with income
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: smarter-air-wing_20260914, commander-priorities_20260914
- **Decision**: (1) The air fund's ceiling is the larger of the one-airframe rule and the cheapest wanted airframe times the number the open air demands are short, that number capped by the room left under the airborne ceiling. (2) The airborne ceiling is the configured value (20) as a floor plus one aircraft per `AirborneIncomePerAirframe` (15) of income a minute, up to `AirborneCeilingMax` (60), read from the same points-plus-mines income the diagnostics line prints.
- **Rationale**: With the count cap removed (DECISION-017) both commanders still bought one to two aircraft a review with thousands banked: the fund's ceiling was the price of ONE dearest airframe (`air saved 61 (cap 126)` beside `budget 8253`), and the player's side sat at `ceiling 20/20` with 34 CAP requests open. The user's standing instruction is "economy limited, not a hard cap".
- **Alternatives**: Raising the flat ceiling (rejected: a poor commander would then overfly its means); a balance-based ceiling (rejected: the balance is what the ladder is spending, so it would oscillate).
- **Impact**: `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAir.cs` (five-argument `AirFundCeiling`, live overload reads `ReadAirDemandCounts`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsAir.cs` (`AirborneCeilingFor`, `EffectiveAirborneCeiling`), every ceiling reader in the buyer and ladder, two settings, ten self-check cases.

### DECISION-019: Idle-pool cap on automatic deployment; downed pilots auto-recovered
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: pickets-first_20260914 (pool), new core service (pilots)
- **Decision**: (1) The game's depot deployment loop is held for a commander the operations service manages once its idle pool reaches `PoolIdleCap` (12); factory supply banks at the depot. (2) A new core-tier service sweeps every 15 s and hands any AI pilot on the ground for `DownedPilotRescueMinutes` (5) to `PilotDismounted.Capture` by the nearest friendly ground unit, the game's own rescue path. Human pilots are excluded.
- **Rationale**: User report: "frame-rate has slowly decayed ... a lot of downed pilots waiting for rescue that should slowly auto-clear up". Decompiled `Factory.ProduceUnit` adds supply, not vehicles; the depot loop then spawns whatever supply exists, and the pool reached 238 idle. `PilotDismounted` has no timeout of its own: a landed pilot persists until captured or killed.
- **Alternatives**: Despawning idle vehicles (rejected: destroys paid-for assets); killing landed pilots (rejected: reads as a loss and the game's own despawn is a plain destroy); stopping the AI from building factories (deferred: factories are a legitimate economy choice, the leak was the deployment).
- **Impact**: `Depot/CommanderFactionVehicleService.cs` (`DeploymentHeldForFullPool`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsRequisitions.cs` (`PoolCount`), new `Core/CommanderDownedPilotService.cs`, two settings, ten self-check cases.

### DECISION-020: Fewer, reachable control points; depot reach; FOBs extend reach; spawn at the nearest depot
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: reach-and-points_20260914
- **Decision**: (1) Discovery drops wooded points and points more than 2 km from a road, resource sites included, bases exempt; the cap falls to 48 points at 3 km spacing and per-point income triples. (2) A point farther than 20 km from every owned vehicle depot is out of reach: no ground staffing, helicopter insertion only. ### DECISION-022: Strategic points are carried across a hot reload, not rediscovered
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Track**: strategic-points_20260913 follow-up
- **Decision**: The strategic-points service implements `ICommanderPersistState`. A snapshot carries every discovered point (kind, position, radius, label, wooded mark, a base's airbase display name, a site's mine by `PersistentID`) with its hold owner stored as a FACTION NAME rather than an `hqOrder` index, plus the retained road polylines flattened to x/y/z triples. A restore rebuilds the list, relinks the airbase by display name and the mine by id, resolves the owner name back to an index against a freshly refreshed `hqOrder`, and sets discovery to Done. - **Rationale**: User, 2026-09-14: "every rebuild moves objectives around and we lose existing platoons". - **Impact**: new `Points/CommanderStrategicPointPersist.cs` (`Snapshot`, `Restore`, `ToRecord`/`FromRecord` for a point and a road polyline, `OwnerIndexByName`, `FactionNameAt`, `IsWaitingForRestore`, `CommanderStrategicPointRecord`, `CommanderRoadPolylineRecord`), `Points/CommanderStrategicPointService.cs` (restore grace in `TickPersistent`, `ResetPersistState` in `ResetSession`, a string `Expect` overload), `Core/CommanderStateModels.cs` (two snapshot properties), `Core/CommanderStateStore.cs` (`IsHotReloadLoad` made internal so the check is not defined a third time). ### DECISION-029: The buyer counts the vehicles it already has, once, where they physically are
- **Date**: 2026-09-15
- **Track**: commander-priorities_20260914 / pickets-first_20260914 follow-up (vehicle buy/sell churn)
- **Decision**: Before the ground buyer orders, each role's open order-book total is reduced by what already covers it — vehicles bought earlier this review, factory supply banked at the depots for the buyer's catalogue definitions, and vehicles idle in the pool — paid off most urgent bucket first (`OpenRolesByPriority`, `PayOffCoverage`). The buyer's view sums each line's full `Wanted` and no longer subtracts the line's `Filled` tally, and the standing-reserve line no longer subtracts the pool total itself; a claimed vehicle is counted exactly once, as a pooled one. The idle-pool sale keeps back as many vehicles of a role as the book has open lines for (`PoolUnitWanted`, counted not blanket), and `PoolIdleSince` is pruned with the staging posts whenever a vehicle leaves the pool.
- **Rationale**: The 2026-09-15 match log showed three quarters of bought vehicles sold unused a minute later. Only `boughtThisReview` was subtracted and it is cleared each review, so an open line was re-bought every 30 s up to five a review; buys that banked supply never registered and never credited the line; the depot-side pool-full hold banked supply that flooded out when platoons formed; and a vehicle returning from a dissolved platoon carried its hour-old idle stamp into the same review's sale. - **Alternatives**: Keeping `Filled` in the buyer's view (rejected: the stall above); a per-role "in flight" counter decremented on claim (rejected: registration is synchronous with the spawn, so nothing falls between bought and pooled/banked, and a spawn that never registers would pin the counter); a blanket skip of every pool vehicle whose role has an open line (rejected: pins a pool over the cap, which now also holds the buyer, with no exit); removing the idle stamp at each of the five pool-removal sites (rejected: `PruneStagingPosts` already runs twice per review with a reused scratch list and is behaviour-equivalent).
- **Impact**: `Operations/CommanderOperationsRequisitions.cs` (`OpenRolesByPriority` signature, `Coverage`, `OpenAfterCoverage`, `PayOffCoverage`, `SumCoverage`, `SumOrderBookByUrgency`, `PostWithdrawingRequisitions`, `PruneStagingPosts`/`PruneNotInPool`, `CheckCoverage`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsService.cs` (`OperationsState.CoveredByRole`, `PoolUnitWanted`, `SellSurplusPool`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs` (self-checks), `Operations/CommanderOperationsDiagnostics.cs` (`covered:` fields), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderService.cs` (pool-full hold, `CommanderState.GroundPoolFull`, catalogue passed to the book).

### DECISION-030: Air attrition brake — escalate the tier, then halve the ceiling and hold the dying side
- **Date**: 2026-09-15
- **Track**: smarter-air-wing_20260914 (follow-up)
- **Decision**: Each commander keeps a ten-minute record of airframe launches and losses per side of the wing (fighter; strike, which includes helicopter CAS and suppression). ### DECISION-039c: One shared idea of a belt — the hold asks the question the sweep asks
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: air-survival-layer_20260916 (amends Layer 3 of DECISION-039)
- **Decision**: "A belt worth suppressing" is one pure rule, `BeltWorthSuppressing(clusteredLaunchers, clusterMinimum)`, living in `Operations/CommanderOperationsAirArad.cs` and read by both callers: `AradWanted`, which sizes the anti-radiation sortie, and `BeltHoldsSortie`, which decides whether a formation holds short of its objective. ### DECISION-042: A marching platoon or a driving picket is re-raised from a depot that has overtaken it, at no cost
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: none (direct user instruction, applied outside a track)
- **Decision**: ONE rule and one action with two callers. A platoon in `Moving` on a `ForwardBase` mission, and a `Picket` mission's ground-driving detachment (`PicketMembers`), are both removed from the map and raised again from the nearest owned depot when the same pure rule says the move is worth it: `CommanderOperationsService.ShouldReseatGroup(remainingDriveMinutes, depotDriveMinutes, savingMinutes, farMinutes, inContact, withdrawing, onCooldown)`. The saving must EXCEED `CommanderSettings.PlatoonReseatSavingMinutes` (new, 15) — exactly on the margin the group is left alone, the patient side of the boundary `PlatoonFlies` and `GroundLineAllowed` already keep — and the group must still be further out than `CommanderSettings.AirMobileDriveMinutes`, which is the mod's ONE idea of a drive that is too long and is also what keeps a group at or near its objective out of the rule. A group in contact (the platoon's `InContactUntil`, the picket mission's `ContactUntil`), one withdrawing, and one inside the thirty-minute `ReseatCooldownSeconds` churn guard (`OnReseatCooldown`) are never dissolved. A fifth term is the strict-progress guard (user decision 2026-09-16, after the rule fired 230 times across 24 groups in one match): the new depot must be STRICTLY nearer the objective than `CommanderReseatRecord.LastDepotMinutes`, the depot drive time the last re-raise moved this group to. Every re-raise therefore shortens the remaining journey, which makes a two-depot loop impossible by construction rather than merely slow. `CommanderReseatRecord.ResetForNewObjective` drops that memory wherever a group is given a new objective, which is `AttachPlatoon` for a platoon and never for a picket, whose objective is its own control point. The cooldown is kept alongside it: one limits how OFTEN a group is moved, the other limits WHERE it may be moved to. Both drive times come from the existing `DriveMinutes` with the existing `RoadDetourFactor` and `GroundSpeedMetersPerSecond`; the depot comes from the existing `CommanderEconomyService.TryNearestOwnedDepot`; the group's position comes from the existing `FirstLiveMember`. The shared decision is `TryPlanReseat` and the shared action is `ReseatGroup`; the bookkeeping is one `CommanderReseatRecord` held by both `CommanderPlatoon` and `CommanderOperationsMission`.
  **Money**: nothing is charged and nothing is refunded, for either caller. Each vehicle is removed through `CommanderEconomyService.DespawnUnit` (the mod's one definition of removing a unit from the game) and the SAME vehicle is handed back to the new depot — `VehicleDepot.TrySpawnVehicle` where the pad is free, otherwise `hq.ModifyUnitSupply(definition, +1)` plus `CommanderFactionVehicleService.ReserveDeployment` naming that depot, which is precisely what the commander's own ground buy does when a pad is busy (`Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderService.cs`). `ReseatVehiclesInTransit` — banked minus returned, for the length of the existing five-minute deployment reservation — is read in four places so the transfer is invisible to the rest of the service: the empty-platoon sweep in `AssignPlatoons` keeps a platoon alive while its vehicles are between despawn and redeploy; `PostForwardBaseRequisitions` and `PostPicketRequisitions` count them as present so the buyer does not purchase the same group a second time; and `FillOnePicket` counts them as present so a re-raised picket does not immediately claim another far vehicle out of the pool and drive the same journey again.
  **What differs between the two callers**: only the container and the follow-up. A platoon is a standing object with a state, a leader and formation slots, so `AfterPlatoonReseat` puts it into `Forming` — the state this service already means by "gathering, not retreating", which keeps the losses rule off it while it is empty — beside the new depot on a `ChooseFormUpPoint`, and `ReleaseReseatedPlatoon` marches it out again on the existing `IsReadyToMoveOut`/`FormUpTimeoutSeconds` pair. A picket has no state machine at all: emptying `PicketMembers` IS the re-raise, and the existing `FillPickets` claims vehicles back out of the pool nearest-first as they redeploy. The log line names which: `re-raises 9TH PLATOON from FOB HILLTOP 5: …` and `re-raises the picket for RESOURCE SITE 25 from FOB HILLTOP 5: …`.
- **Rationale**: User, 2026-09-16: "we have situations where platoons are bought and sent to the enemy, but often a FOB is built closer to the enemy long before they're anywhere near. when that happens, the platoon should despawn and then be re-spawned from that FOB", and the same day: "platoon re-raising should also apply to pickets travelling by ground." The evidence is in the running log — `raises 9TH PLATOON air-mobile for HILLTOP 13: 40 km from the nearest depot, 58 min by road` — where the air-mobile gate already answers the same question for a group that has not been raised yet. This is that gate's ground twin for one already under way, deliberately sharing its threshold rather than introducing a second idea of "far". Selling the vehicles through the idle-pool path (`PoolSellRefundFraction`, 0.5) and re-buying would have cost half a platoon for moving one; buying replacements outright would have cost a whole one. Neither is honest when the commander already owns the vehicles.
- **Alternatives**: Two implementations, one per caller (rejected outright — Reuse rule 4; the margin, the far test, the contact guard, the churn guard and the money accounting are identical and only the container differs); refunding at the idle-pool sale fraction and letting the order book re-buy (rejected: a 50% loss every time a forward base opens, which would make the feature cost more than the drive it saves); leaving a re-raised platoon in `Moving` with zero members while the vehicles redeploy (rejected: the losses rule reads a one-vehicle platoon as below half strength and would send it home the review the first vehicle arrived); giving a picket the platoon's Forming/Moving treatment (rejected as paraphrasing one caller into the other — a picket genuinely has no state to come out of, and `FillPickets` already does the re-claim); including `Attack` missions (rejected: an attack's platoons are bound to an axis, a release point and a form-up timeout, and dissolving one mid-approach is a behaviour change nobody asked for); a per-base "re-raised once for this base" flag instead of a cooldown (rejected: it needs a second record keyed on depots that come and go, where a clock longer than the deployment reservation gives the same guarantee).
- **Impact**: new `Operations/CommanderOperationsReseat.cs` (`ReseatCooldownSeconds`, `ReseatInTransitSeconds`, `ShouldReseatGroup`, `OnReseatCooldown`, `ReseatNoPreviousDepot`, `ReseatRecordAfterNewObjective`, `ReseatVehiclesInTransit` and its record overload, `ReviewGroundReseats`, `TryPlanReseat`, `ReseatGroup`, `AfterPlatoonReseat`, `ReleaseReseatedPlatoon`, `CheckReseat`); `Operations/CommanderPlatoon.cs` (new `CommanderReseatRecord`, held by both `CommanderPlatoon` and `CommanderOperationsMission`); `Operations/CommanderOperationsService.cs` (the review call, the self-check call); `Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs` (the empty-platoon sweep guard, `FillOnePicket`'s wanted count, the strict-progress reset in `AttachPlatoon`); `Operations/CommanderOperationsRequisitions.cs` (the forward base's free-slot count, the picket's shortfall); `Core/CommanderSettings.cs` (`PlatoonReseatSavingMinutes`, 15, plus its warm-up touch); `CHANGELOG.md`.

### DECISION-045: A sortie's strength is what is assigned to it, it never holds over its own runway, every kind goes in whole, and every size is read through one envelope
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: sortie-commitment_20260916 (answers `conductor/designs/2026-09-16-sortie-standdown-investigation.md`; builds on `air-fallback-posture_20260916` and `air-survival-layer_20260916`)

The line is `<sortie>: no clear ground to hold — its fighters join the home patrol.` **(3)** `SortieIsPackage` is widened in place into `SortieFormsUp(kind, casWanted, capWanted, groundContact, aradGoneIn)` and the whole existing form-up machinery — `TryFindFormUpPoint`, `CountAtFormUp`, `PackageGoesIn`, `CommanderSettings.PackageFormUpSeconds` — now serves fighter patrols, ground-attack sorties and escorts as well as strike packages. The decision is taken ONCE, in `AddDemand`, and stored in `GoneIn`; `SortieHoldsAtFormUp` reads the stored answer rather than re-deriving it from counts the sizing rebuilds every thirty seconds. The reconcile's `wanted.GoneIn = live.GoneIn || wanted.GoneIn` is the way out of a hold. **(4)** `SortieElementWanted(floor, grown, cap)` is the one sizing envelope, generalised from `StrikeEscortWanted` and read by the escort rule, `CapWanted`, `CasWanted` and the falling-back sortie's `CallForFighters`. The first three retrofits are behaviour-neutral and a self-check loop pins that at every input from 0 to 12; the fourth gains a cap, the new class constant `FallbackFightersCap` = `2 * CapPerObjectiveCap` = 6.
- **The in-contact judgement, and why**: a sortie whose objective is in GROUND contact never forms up, and a sortie already gathering goes in the moment its objective comes into ground contact. Hostile AIRCRAFT tracked near the objective are deliberately NOT an exemption. `SortieIsInContact` reads true whenever anything hostile is tracked in the ring, so exempting on it would switch Part 3 off in precisely the fights it exists for — and the 96 sorties that ended `its fighters are gone` are what trickling fighters into hostile air costs. Ground contact is different in kind: a platoon under fire cannot wait three minutes, and `RetaskSourceRank` already ranks ground contact above cover. Both directions carry a self-check. The accepted cost is that a patrol over a quiet objective may now spend up to `PackageFormUpSeconds` (180 s) assembling where it used to be on station in about a minute.
- **Why the fallback call is capped rather than clamped by headroom**: `StrikeEscortWanted` clamps the escort by room under the airborne ceiling, and the same clamp on a patrol would have been wrong. Sortie demand also drives the RETASK, which moves aircraft that are already airborne and already under the ceiling, so a headroom clamp would stop a contested objective drawing fighters off quiet ones at exactly the moment the sky is full. The airborne ceiling is already enforced where aircraft are actually bought. A per-sortie cap answers the real absurdity, which was a median call for eleven fighters and one for thirty-one.
- **What was deliberately NOT changed**: what a QUIET objective asks for. The design's prose says "a quiet objective draws a pair"; the user's instruction says not to silently change it. It stays at one fighter and no ground-attack airframe, kept as the envelope's floor, with two self-checks pinning it. ### DECISION-050: Cargo transports unload in place on every delivery, bounded by the clock that already bounds the hover
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: delivery-bypass_20260916 (design approved by the user; survey `conductor/designs/2026-09-16-transport-delivery-survey.md`)
- **Problem**: the game releases cargo only when radar altitude is under 2 m AND speed is under 10 m/s, while inside 300 m of the touchdown point it switches flight assist off and commands a hover at 20 m (`AIHeloTransportState`, survey Part 2 steps 6-7). A transport holding that hover never satisfies the gate. Nine flights in three measured play blocks hovered long enough to trip the stall clock, and the VL-49 Tarantula tiltwing ended 29 percent of its losses in the ground against the UH-90 Ibis's 9 percent, every attributable crash at the destination.
- **Decision (A) - EVERY delivery, not only stalled ones.** The user was shown that hovering is rare (9 of 80 flights) and already bounded by the stall clock, and that the two larger buckets are 18 shot down in transit and 22 recalled by our own route watch, which the bypass does nothing for. They chose the bypass anyway, on the grounds that predictable delivery is worth having on its own. Recorded here so it is not re-litigated; the two larger causes are named as separate work in the design's section 6 and were deliberately not touched, because changing the route watch's recall rule or the escort in the same change would confound the measurement.
- **Decision (B) - the unload condition is one rule with two ceilings.** `CommanderCargoUnloadRule.UnloadsInPlace(metres, radarAlt, speed, ring, ceiling, speedLimit)`: inside `CommanderOperationsService.InsertionStallRadiusMeters` (500 m, the stall clock's own ring), at or below the ceiling, and strictly under the speed limit. `UnloadHeightMeters` is 25 - it MUST clear the game's 20 m hover, because a lower ceiling would almost never be met and every delivery would fall through to the bounded wait, which is the hang being removed; a named self-check pins that. `UnloadSpeedMetersPerSecond` is 10, the game's own gate speed, kept deliberately unchanged so exactly one of the two gate numbers moved.
- **Decision (C) - the bounded wait reuses the existing stall clock, and adds no new clock and no new setting.** The risk in "must also be low" is a transport that cannot descend and therefore never unloads. The stall clock the operations side has run since 2026-09-14 - `HasStalledAtLandingZone` with `OperationsInsertionStallTimeoutSeconds` (120 s) inside the same 500 m ring, in `Operations/CommanderOperationsInsertion.cs` and its twin in `Operations/CommanderOperationsFob.cs` - now calls `TryForceUnloadInPlace` FIRST, which applies the same rule with `ForcedUnloadHeightMeters` (40). A refusal falls through to the two answers those branches have always given: the parachute conversion, then the recall. So a transport delivers, drops or is recalled, and can never hang. A flight that has ALREADY latched answers the clock with `UnloadIsProgressing`: yes while it is still putting vehicles out, because a normal unload can easily still be running when a clock that started on arrival expires, and no after `UnloadProgressGraceSeconds` (30 s) of silence. The `no` is the load-bearing half - an unconditional yes would reset the stall clock for ever and rebuild this track's own hang one layer up, and a `no` hands the flight to the parachute drop and then the recall, the same rescue every other stuck flight gets. The forced call is quoted with the flight's own slot (`CommanderCargoFlightSlot.Matches`), so a wave unloads the load that could not get down and not the one beside it.
- **Decision (D) - spacing is the release cadence's, because the ramp-clear handshake genuinely breaks.** The handshake exists to drive the first vehicle clear before the second is released, and there is no ramp on the ground to clear when the transport never touched down. A bypassed load therefore takes the branch an airdrop takes, and the existing `NextCargoReleaseAt` / `ReleasedCargoCount` / `ActivatedCargoCount` fields do the work: one vehicle at a time, each waiting for the last to leave the aircraft, with the routine's existing 2.5 s gap. On top of that each vehicle gets its own ground, 30 m apart on the four cardinal bearings, measured from where the transport was when it latched (`CargoMission.UnloadAnchor`) rather than from where it is at each release: the unload speed limit is 10 m/s and roughly five seconds pass between releases, so a live reading could put the second vehicle back on the first. The spacing is exact between the WANTED spots only - where the only clearing for a hundred metres is one clearing, both can still be sent to it, and that is deliberately left to the insertion shield, which is what the ramp-clear handshake protected against in the first place.
- **Decision (E) - the vehicles are set down, not dropped.** Clear ground comes from `TryFindClearLandingZone` (`Supply/CommanderSupplyHeliLandingZone.cs`), the search the mod already uses and the only one it has; nothing clear inside the radius falls back to the terrain directly beneath, because awkward ground beats no delivery. The move itself is `PlaceVehicleUpright`, extracted from the insertion shield's existing `RightVehicle` under Reuse rule 5, and runs from the shield sweep rather than the cargo-activation hook - the sweep already moves delivered vehicles every tick and is proven, and moving one inside the frame the engine is still activating it in would be new risk in the delivery path with the worst incident history in this repository.
- **Confirmed before relying on it (design section 4.4)**: firing a cargo mount already empties the aircraft. `MountedCargo.Fire` sets `fired = true` and `ammo = 0`, and `MountedCargo.GetAmmoLoaded()` returns 0 once `fired` - the very accessor `HasDeployableCargo` and `TrySelectNextCargoWeapon` read - so `DeployNextAssignedCargo` reaches its own `IssueInsertionReturnToBase` branch with nothing new written. Verified in the decompiled `Assembly-CSharp`, not assumed.
- **Correction to the design's section 4.7.** The design says the airframe fate line is written only for the computer enemy commander because the tracking service "skips the local faction". It does not skip it: `TaskAirWing` runs for every commanded HQ including the player's. The real defect is that `airborneSince` is ONE table shared by every faction and `ReportLostAircraft(hq)` drained all of it under whichever HQ the review loop reached first. Measured in the BepInEx log: 1,227 fate lines, every one attributed to Primeva (the enemy), none to Boscali (the player), and 199 "recovered" lines likewise - so the player's transports were unmeasured AND the enemy's counts were inflated by the player's losses. Each tracked airframe now carries its owner and each commander reports only its own.
- **Alternatives**: a narrow rescue on the stall clock only, leaving normal deliveries alone (the survey's own ranked option 2 - overridden by the user's decision (A), and it is exactly what `TryForceUnloadInPlace` now is, kept as the second half rather than the whole); dropping the vehicles and letting them fall under the shield (rejected: the user's earlier complaint was vehicles "dropped from a too-high height and dying on contact", and setting them down removes the fall entirely); a second clear-ground search near the post rather than near the transport (rejected by design section 4.3 and Reuse rule 1 - there is one such search in this mod and there will stay one); a new stall clock owned by the supply side (rejected by design section 4.2 - one clock, and it already exists); routing every threshold through the settings window (rejected: each is pinned to a number in the game's own code, so they are class constants with their reasons, and the one setting added is an off switch for the whole bypass).
- **Impact**: `Supply/CommanderCargoUnloadRule.cs` (new - the decision table and its named checks, with no Unity, Harmony or game-assembly dependency so it can be driven outside a running game, for the same reason `CommanderCargoFlightSlot` has none); `Supply/CommanderSupplyHeliUnload.cs` (new - `BypassesLanding`, `TickUnloadInPlace`, `BeginUnloadInPlace`, `TryForceUnloadInPlace`, `TryChooseUnloadGround`, `SelfCheckUnload`); `Supply/CommanderSupplyHeliMission.cs` (one call in `OverrideTransportTarget`; `CargoMission.UnloadInPlace` / `.UnloadGroundFallbackLogged` / `.UnloadPlacedCount`; the two ramp-clear starts and the take-off hold each gain one term; `ShieldDeliveredCargo` told where to place; `UpdateDeliveryCompleted` routed through the shared rule); `Supply/CommanderSupplyHeliShield.cs` (`PlaceVehicleUpright` extracted from `RightVehicle`; `ShieldRecord.PlaceAt`; the sweep acts on it; `SelfCheckUnload` registered through the existing `SelfCheck`); `Operations/CommanderOperationsInsertion.cs` and `Operations/CommanderOperationsFob.cs` (the forced unload tried before the airdrop conversion in both stall branches); `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAirTasking.cs` and `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderService.cs` (`TrackedAirframe.Owner`); `Core/CommanderSettings.cs` (`SupplyUnloadInPlaceEnabled` and its warm-up touch); `CHANGELOG.md`. ### DECISION-051: One difficulty slider scales both the opposition's income and its opening balance
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: none (direct user instruction, 2026-09-16: "i think we need to add a difficulty slider to add funds / scale income to the opposition." Asked to choose, the user said the slider scales BOTH income and starting funds, and that it reads as a plain multiplier with the number shown rather than as named steps like Easy or Hard.)
- **Problem**: the handicap already existed but only half of it, and it was invisible. `CommanderSettings.EnemyIncomeMultiplier` (added 2026-09-15) scaled every income a non-local faction earned, through `CommanderEconomyService.IncomeHandicap` / `IncomeHandicapFor`, but it could only be changed by editing the BepInEx configuration file, and it did nothing to the balance a faction opens a match on. Opening balances were set by two openers with no handicap at all: `LevelEconomy` copies the player's authored balance onto an opposing faction on a stock mission, and `PrepareDuel` multiplies an opposing faction's balance by `DuelHeadStart` (1.5) on the mod's own duel map.
- **Answer to the question the brief asked**: the existing 1.5x duel head start does NOT reach the player's faction. `CommanderEnemyCommanderService.Review` computed `isLocal` against the HQ the loop was handed (`CommanderGameAccess.GetLocalHq()`, the same reference the income handicap uses) and skipped BOTH openers for it, logging "keeps the player's own economy: no head start, no fund reset." That was already correct before this change and is unchanged by it; the only thing that moved is that the skip is now expressed as a named pure function.
- **Decision (A) - the two halves share one rule, they are not two settings.** `CommanderEconomyService.StartingFundsFor(baseFunds, isLocal, multiplier)` is `Mathf.Max(0f, baseFunds * IncomeHandicapFor(isLocal, multiplier))`. It is deliberately built ON the income rule rather than beside it (Reuse rule 4): one definition of who gets handicapped, two callers, so the slider cannot come to mean one thing for income and another for opening balances. `StartingFunds(hq, baseFunds)` is the live wrapper, the same shape as `IncomeHandicap(hq)`. The outer floor is load-bearing on its own - a faction authored into the red opens on nothing rather than deeper in the red - and has its own named check.
- **Decision (B) - the multiplier is applied once, after the openers, not inside them.** `ApplyDifficultyToOpeningFunds(hq)` runs last in the prepare block, on whatever the openers left in the balance. Applying it inside both `LevelEconomy` and `PrepareDuel` was rejected because the duel runs both and the multiplier would have been squared. Applying it only inside `PrepareDuel` was rejected because stock missions never call it, so the slider would have scaled income everywhere and opening balances only on the duel map. Applying it after both also means it scales the balance the MISSION authored when neither opener runs, so the slider means the same thing on every mission. At the default of 1 the method returns before calling `SetFunds` at all, so the default is not merely equivalent to today's behaviour, it is the same code path.
- **Decision (C) - the once-per-match rule is a pure function, so it is checkable.** `CommanderEconomyService.ShouldOpenTreasury(isLocal, alreadyPrepared)` is `!isLocal && !alreadyPrepared` and is now the gate on the whole prepare block in `Review`, replacing the `if (!state.Prepared) { if (!isLocal) ... }` nest. `CommanderState.Prepared` is cleared only by `ResetSession` (a new match or a hot reload), so moving the slider mid-match changes income from the next payout onwards and cannot hand out a second opening balance. Four named checks pin it, including both "already prepared" cases.
- **Decision (D) - the gate and the checks live in the economy service, not in the commander.** They started in `CommanderEnemyCommanderService` next to `PrepareDuel`, which is where the head start is. They were moved because that class cannot be type-initialised outside the game: its static constructor runs `AccessTools.FieldRefAccess<GroundVehicle, bool>("commandedDestination")` (`Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderDefence.cs`), which throws `TypeLoadException: Could not load type 'SteeringInfo'` in a headless harness. Putting the whole difficulty rule - who, how much, how often - beside the income half it already shares a multiplier with is the better home anyway, and it is what makes the rule provable outside the game.
- **Decision (E) - the slider shares a row rather than taking one.** The GAMEPLAY tab's COMMAND box already reaches 784 px of a 790 px window with the help overlay open (its own comment records the arithmetic), so a tenth full-width row does not fit. The capture slider's label shortens to "Aircraft capture"; its range, snapping and behaviour are unchanged. Rejected alternatives: wrapping the whole GAMEPLAY tab in a scroll view as the POINTS tab does (correct in the long run, but a full-tab layout rewrite for one slider); raising the 790 px window limit (changes every tab); pairing two of the nine toggles to free a row (more churn on controls this change has no business touching).
- **Decision (F) - range 0.5x to 3.0x, snapped to 0.05, shown as a number.** `EnemyDifficultyMin` is 0.5 because below half an opposing commander cannot accumulate the price of the cheapest platoon between two reviews and simply stops buying, which reads as broken rather than easy. `EnemyDifficultyMax` is 3 because past three the opposition fills the map faster than the player can cross it, and because 1 then sits inside the range rather than at its end. A named check pins that the range still brackets 1. Named steps were rejected by the user.

### DECISION-052: A base with no runway launches and recovers helicopters only
- **Date**: 2026-09-16
- **Track**: none (direct user instruction, 2026-09-16: "we need to modify it so that FOBs (their helipads) can only produce helicopters - if it tries to spawn VT-7s (which are VTOL) the AI fails to know what to do with them and it just despawns or crashes.")
- **Problem**: every place the mod asked whether a base would produce a type delegated to the game's `Airbase.CanSpawnAircraft` / `Hangar.CanSpawnAircraft`. Both are pure roster lookups in the decompiled `Assembly-CSharp` — `Hangar` holds an authored `availableAircraft` array and `Airbase.CanSpawnAircraft` is the union over its hangars — so a helipad answers yes for every airframe its prefab roster names. The VT-7 Vagrant (`VTOLTrainer1`) is on that roster and is a vertical-lift airframe carrying a `Pilot.PilotType.Plane`, so `Pilot.SetStartingAiState` gives it `AIPilotCombatModes`, the aeroplane AI that expects a strip, and it is then started on a forward base that has none. This is NOT the `PilotType.VTOL` case `CommanderAirCommandService.CanAiFly` already bans, which is exactly why it got through. The cheap-aircraft work of the same day made the bottom tier, which holds the Vagrant, far more likely to be bought.
- **Decision (A) - one rule, stated as a facility rule, not as a base-type rule.** `CommanderAirLaunchFacility.FacilitySuitsAirframe(baseHasRunway, airframeIsRotary)` is `baseHasRunway || airframeIsRotary`. It takes two booleans and no game types, so the whole decision table can be driven outside a running game, the shape `CommanderAirCommandGroundLoss.CheckGroundLoss` established.
- **Decision (B) - "flies like a helicopter" is not re-decided.** The rule reads `CommanderAirCommandService.IsRotaryAirframe`, the mod's existing definition-level test (`PilotType.Helo` or `PilotType.Tiltwing`, read off the prefab). Reuse rule 4: one definition, and the enemy commander, the player's AIR window and the supply side all read the same one. No second idea of what a helicopter is was written.
- **Decision (C) - the tiltwing VL-49 Tarantula MAY launch from a helipad.** It is rotary by `IsRotaryAirframe`, the game gives it `AIHeloCombatState` rather than the aeroplane AI, and it recovers by homing on `airbase.verticalLandingPoints`, which a helipad provides and a runway does not. Excluding it would have needed a second rotary test that disagreed with the rest of the mod, and would have stopped forward bases and air-mobile lifts using their most capable transport. Its wing-borne entry problem is a separate, already-fixed concern (`IsTiltwingAirframe`, the spawn speed), not a facility problem. A named check pins that a pad-only base still launches it.
- **Decision (D) - a base is pad-only when it has no runway, read off the base itself.** `CommanderAirLaunchFacility.BaseHasRunway` walks `airbase.runways` and accepts any runway with both end transforms. Chosen over the two alternatives the brief offered. "Does a hangar here report a non-rotary type" is circular: a helipad's authored roster naming the Vagrant IS the defect, so every helipad would classify as an airfield. Matching the mod's own forward-base records (`CommanderOperationsFob`, the `FobBasePrefix` name) is a name test, and breaks on a map that names things differently, and would say nothing about a destroyer's deck pad. The runway test is derived from what the facility HAS: `CommanderFobBuilder.TryCreateFobAirbase` creates a forward base with an empty runway list on purpose ("a FOB has no strip"), `Airbase.SetupAttachedAirbase` leaves a ship's prefab-authored runways alone so a carrier deck still counts as a strip (its `Runway` carries a `Rigidbody` and a `GetVelocity`, which only a moving deck needs), and a ship with only a pad has none. Runways' `Takeoff` and `Landing` flags are deliberately NOT read, because one test has to give launch and recovery the same answer or an airframe is produced somewhere it can never come home to.
- **Decision (E) - one wrapper, and the raw game call is made in exactly one file.** `CanBaseLaunch(airbase, definition)` and `CanHangarLaunch(airbase, hangar, definition)` are the only readers of `Airbase.CanSpawnAircraft` and `Hangar.CanSpawnAircraft` in the mod. Every former call site was converted and carries a pointer comment saying why it does not call the game directly: `AirCommand/CommanderAirCommandMissions.cs` (the AIR window's base list, its launch gate, and `TryFindClearHangar` — which also folded its `Disabled`/`Available` readiness pair into `CanHangarLaunch`), `AirCommand/CommanderAirCommandRecovery.cs` (`ChooseRelaunchAirbase`, both the origin and the nearest-other branch), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAirBuy.cs` (the pinned-base acceptance), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAirCatalog.cs` (`NearestRotaryLaunchMeters` and `FindAcceptingAirbase`), `Supply/CommanderSupplyHeliService.cs` (`IsAvailableAirbase`, the one gate the whole supply side already passes through). No call site kept the raw answer.
- **Decision (F) - the buy side stops OFFERING, not just refusing.** No extra code was needed: `FindAcceptingAirbase` is the converted site, and `RefreshAirCatalog` already drops any type for which it returns null, so a type no held base can launch leaves the enemy commander's catalogue rather than being bought and then refused at the pad.
- **Recovery is included on purpose.** `ChooseRelaunchAirbase` was using `CanSpawnAircraft` as "does this base handle this type", and a relaunch from a base whose facilities do not suit the airframe is the same defect one step later. The facility rule is stated for launch AND recovery for that reason.
- **The reverse mismatch exists but is not the reported bug, and was NOT fixed here.** Nothing stops a helicopter being produced at a runway-only field with no vertical landing point. It is much milder: the mod launches airborne (`TryLaunchAiAircraft`), so the take-off is fine, and two guards already cover the dangerous half — `CommanderAirCommandService.RequestReturnToBase` and the RTB state switch both refuse to send a rotary airframe home unless `HasVerticalPad` finds one, because `AIHeloLandingState` ejects the crew when it finds no pad at all, and `CommanderFobBuilder.TryGetBaseWithoutPad` puts a pad on any held base that lacks one near the top of the building wish list. The gap that remains is that `HasVerticalPad` asks the whole faction, not the base the airframe launched from, so a helicopter can be produced at a padless field and have to fly to another base to land. Left for the user to decide, since closing it would change where helicopters are bought.

### DECISION-053: Strategic save and load — the picture survives a mission restart, units are cashed in, garrisons and economy buildings are bought back
- **Date**: 2026-09-17
- **Track**: `strategic-save_20260917` (study: `conductor/designs/2026-09-17-save-load-study.md`, which was rewritten mid-track; this entry follows the rewritten version)
- **Problem**: the frame rate decays over a long match and the only cure is restarting the mission. The game has no in-match save — `MissionSaveLoad` writes a mission DEFINITION and nothing writes live state back — so a restart throws the match away. The mod's own snapshot framework could not help: `CommanderStateStore.TryRestore` is only reached when `IsHotReloadLoad` is true, and `PassesSessionGuard` demands the saved level time be earlier than and within 600 s of the current one. A mission restart is not a hot reload and starts the level clock at zero, so BOTH halves fail and nothing would load at all.
- **Decision (A) — a second store, not a widened one.** `Core/CommanderStrategicSaveStore.cs` writes `<mission>.strategic.json` beside the hot-reload file and reads it through `PassesStrategicGuard(snapshot, mission)`: mission name plus format version, and deliberately NO time window, because an explicit save the developer took does not expire. The hot-reload path, its gate, its file and its records are unchanged, and it keeps first refusal — the strategic store stands down on a hot-reload run. Fixing the gate was task one for exactly the reason the study gave: until a file can survive a restart at all, nothing else can be tested.
- **Decision (B) — the hot-reload records must NOT cross a restart.** They are keyed by `PersistentID`, a bare counter `UnitRegistry.Clear()` resets, so a saved id names a DIFFERENT unit in the new run. That is why the two record sets are separate rather than one file with two gates. `ICommanderPersistStrategic` is a parallel interface beside `ICommanderPersistState` with the same shape and the same registry fan-out; `CarriesNoUnitIdentifier` refuses a save carrying an identifier, on the way out and on the way in.
- **Decision (C) — no clock crosses a restart either.** Every cooldown in the mod is a `Time.time` stamp and the level clock restarts at zero, so a saved value in the thousands reads as far in the future and silently pauses that behaviour for the match. The defence is structural rather than by value: `CarriesNoReplayedClock` reflects over the strategic record types and refuses any numeric field whose name reads like a clock, with one allowed exception, `SavedLevelTime`, which is written for the log and never read back as a number.
- **Decision (D) — ownership is not a stored fact, so the garrison is what makes it stick.** Holding a control point is re-derived every five seconds from how many vehicles stand in the ring, and `Step` sets the owner back to neutral the moment nobody qualifies. Restoring ownership without placing garrisons would blank the whole map about five seconds after the load, silently, and look like the restore simply did not work. The garrison step is therefore load-bearing, not a nicety, and the checks say so in those terms. Belt and braces, `IsHoldingPoints` holds the hold tick off for 20 s around the rebuild, capped so a failed rebuild releases it rather than freezing the hold machine.
- **Decision (E) — two kinds of point are exempt from that, because their owner is somebody else's state.** `StrategicGarrisonHoldsThisKind(kind, mineStanding)`: an AIRBASE is owned by the game's own capture ring and is handed back with `Capture.ForceCapture`; a SITE with a mine standing on it is owned by the mine, which the economy rebuild has already put back by the time the garrison step runs. A site with NO mine is held by presence like a village and does get one.
- **Decision (F) — units are refunded at their value, never recreated** (user decision). `CommanderEconomyService.StrategicUnitValue(Unit)` is the ONE valuation, over the game's own worth rating `UnitDefinition.value` and the two inline precedents that already refund by it (the idle-pool vehicle sale and the stuck-on-deck aircraft refund). The study's three special cases are all handled and all checked: a catalogue structure carries a cost multiplier and is valued at `GetStructureCost`; a mine, factory or naval dock was bought at a FLAT configured price with its upgrades as sunk cost on top, which `StrategicFlatSunkCost(kind, level)` computes by walking the mod's own upgrade ladders — that figure is NOT what they are cashed in for, since decision G rebuilds them instead, but it is what is credited back on the branch where a rebuild fails; and a building the commander did not build is worth nothing, because a fresh mission puts every mission-authored structure back. A forward base's structures are worth nothing either, because the base comes back in kind. A disabled unit is worth nothing, which is what stops the study's "knocked-out building left in the old faction's list" paying the wrong treasury.
- **Decision (G) — mines, factories and docks are REBUILT and charged nothing, exactly like forward bases** (user decision 2026-09-17, revising my first answer, which cashed them in and bought them back). They are the strategic picture rather than units: a mine is why a resource site earns anything, so cashing them in would collapse each faction's income on every load until the commander rebuilt them — resuming a match differently rather than resuming it. `StrategicRebuiltInKind(kind, forwardBaseBuilding)` is the one place that rule is decided, and it covers forward-base structures and all three economy buildings together, so there is one rule and not two. They are put back through the mod's own `SpawnMine` / `SpawnFactory` / `SpawnNavalDock`, mines first, before either commander starts spending. `StrategicEconomyRebuildCharge` states the price as a function that answers zero, so a later edit that starts charging has to change that line and fail its check.
- **Decision (G1) — upgrade levels ARE restorable, and are restored.** The levels live in the mod's own `mineLevels`, `factoryLevels` and `dockLevels` dictionaries, keyed by the live `Unit`, so the rebuild writes the saved level straight in after the spawn. No fallback was needed. Without this a save-and-load would quietly demote every building the commander had improved, since `SpawnMine` and `SpawnNavalDock` both set level 1 and a fresh factory is absent from its table, which reads as level 1.
- **Decision (G2) — a mine is restored to its own site, and the game is what decides whether that worked.** `SpawnMine` enforces the agreement itself and needed no new code: it snaps to the nearest resource site and REFUSES unless the builder holds that site outright. That is exactly the wanted behaviour — a mine is never restored onto ground the game says is somebody else's — and it is why the economy rebuild must run AFTER the point owners are restored, which the ordered apply guarantees: with the site still neutral the snap refuses and nothing is built. `WarnIfMineDidNotTakeItsSite` then reads the world back and logs when a rebuilt mine did not end up owning its site, so a silent mismatch cannot leave the commander believing in income it does not have.
- **Decision (G3) — a rebuild that fails credits the sunk cost back.** My call, not the developer's, and the only place `StrategicFlatSunkCost` is still used. These buildings are deliberately NOT cashed in, so a rebuild that cannot be placed would otherwise leave the faction poorer by the building's whole price with nothing to show for it. Crediting the flat purchase price plus the upgrades paid on top is the fallback branch, never the normal one, and it is what keeps the conservation property true when the rebuild fails as well as when it succeeds. Forward bases are deliberately NOT given the same credit: their structures were charged at order time as the stake, which is the rule the delivery path already applies.
- **Decision (H) — conservation of value, confirmed by the developer.** The garrisons are bought at the commander's own price from the commander's own catalogue through the commander's own rule: `CommanderEnemyCommanderService.CheapestCaptureVehicle`, extracted from `ChooseCaptureUnit` behaviour-neutrally (Reuse rule 5) and retrofitted, so a garrison and a bought capture unit can never disagree about what can hold ground. Whole or nothing per point. Points are restored in `RankPoints`' own priority order until the money runs out, and a point that cannot be paid for is released to neutral and named in the log with both figures. `StrategicValueConserved(fundsBefore, cashedIn, garrisonSpend, economyRefund, fundsAfter)` states the property the whole track is judged on — a save-and-reload leaves a faction no richer and no poorer apart from what it deliberately spends on garrisons — and is checked in seven scenarios including three failure directions: a garrison handed over free, an economy building charged for on rebuild, and the one the team lead named specifically, a building both cashed in AND rebuilt, which would make a faction richer by the price of its whole economy on every save. `CommanderSettings.StrategicGarrisonPaid` turns the charging off for testing.
- **Decision (I) — a delivering forward base is cancelled, not refunded** (study correction; my earlier refund was wrong). There is no refund anywhere else on the delivery path — "No refund — the structures were charged at order time, which is the stake" — and a restored half-built order would sit stranded until its own fifteen-minute stall timeout killed it, because the transport it was waiting on no longer exists. `StrategicDeliveringRefund` states the rule as a function that answers zero, so a later edit that starts refunding has to change that line and fail its check. **One deliberate narrowing of the study:** it recommended cancelling at SAVE time. I do not cancel a live order, because pressing SAVE must not change a running match; the order is simply absent from the file, and `ReportForwardBasesLostToSave` names it in the log at the moment the save is taken. Same outcome on load, no side effect on the match the developer is still playing.
- **Decision (J) — the forward base's record is synthesised and its two clocks are seeded.** `TryBuildFob` creates a base complete and standalone, charges nothing and reads no order record, so the rebuild is free by construction. Both are stamped from `now`.
- **Decision (K) — the forward-base name counter is seeded past the world.** It is not saved and is zeroed on reset, so after a restart it starts at one again while the restored bases carry their old numbers and the first rebuild would collide. `SeedFobNameCounterFromWorld` walks the held airbases by prefix and pushes the counter past the highest, using the pure `TrailingNumber`.
- **Decision (L) — the prepared flag and the out-of-treasury pots.** `ShouldOpenTreasury` reads `CommanderState.Prepared`, which `ResetSession` clears, so a load that restores the money without the flag has every computer faction's next review re-open its treasury on top of it, invisibly and unwinnably. The saved faction names are applied in `Review` at the moment the per-faction state is created, which is the only point early enough. The naval, air, radar-watch and picket pots and the structure bank are restored with them: not extra money, but dropping them resets every saving-up decision a commander had made.
- **Decision (M) — the restore is load-only, then an ordered apply.** `RestoreStrategic` writes nothing to the world, because the registry fans out in registration order and this order is different: war chest, then the out-of-treasury pots, then the airbases by force capture, then the mines/factories/docks, then the forward bases, then the garrisons. Each step depends on the one before it.
- **Decision (N) — trigger is two buttons, not a key.** A STRATEGIC SAVE box on the POINTS tab: SAVE STRATEGIC STATE, DISCARD SAVE, the paid-garrison toggle and a status line. The developer needs to SEE that a save exists and when it was taken before restarting, and a key binding has nowhere to say that. The button only REQUESTS the save; the file is written on the mod's own persistent tick, so nothing is written from inside IMGUI. A consumed save is renamed to `.strategic.loaded.json` rather than deleted, because reloading the same mission twice while testing is normal and the rename still makes a double-apply impossible.

### DECISION-056: Air insertions wait for their escorts to be in front of them
- **Date**: 2026-09-17
- **Track**: none — direct user instruction, 2026-09-17: "air insertions should wait for their escorts to be ahead of them (they have a habit of flying straight into danger)."
- **Problem**: `LiftMayLaunch` (`Operations/CommanderOperationsAirPlatoonCap.cs`) let a load go as soon as `escortsUp >= escortsWanted`. "Up" is `CountFightersUp`, which counts a cover's bound fixed-wing airframes that are alive — it does not ask whether they are airborne, and it does not ask where they are. A transport therefore launched the moment its fighters had been bought and bound, overtook them, and arrived first and alone over the landing zone.
- **Decision (A) — "ahead" is ground still to cover, never a fraction of the route.** `EscortIsAhead(escortAirborne, escortToLandingZoneMeters, loadToLandingZoneMeters, marginMeters)` is the one pure predicate: the escort is off the deck AND is nearer the landing zone than the load is, by at least the margin. A fraction of the route was rejected by the user's own reasoning: a load and its cover routinely launch from different airbases, and the same fraction of two different routes is not the same place. Inclusive at the boundary, the convention the rest of the mod uses.
- **Decision (B) — airborne is asked explicitly, inside the predicate.** `CountFightersUp` counts a parked fighter, so a cover sitting on the runway of a base nearer the landing zone than the load's own base would have read as "ahead" with its wheels down. Folding the airborne test into the pure predicate rather than into the counting wrapper is what makes it self-checkable; `CommanderAirCommandService.IsOnDeck` is the existing test and is reused rather than re-derived.
- **Decision (C) — the margin is a SETTING, not a constant.** `CommanderSettings.LiftEscortLeadMeters`, default 2,000 m, with its `Get`/`Set` pair and its warm-up touch, and no slider — the same shape as its siblings `LiftEscortMinimum`, `LiftFundsMultiple` and `LiftAirheadMaxMeters`, none of which has one either. A setting because the right lead depends on the transport and fighter mix a mission fields and on the size of the map, which is the kind of number the developer retunes inside a match rather than between builds. Two kilometres is about half a minute of flying for a loaded transport helicopter at roughly 60 m/s and about fifteen seconds of relative motion for a fighter at roughly 200 m/s leaving the same airbase, so the gap is meaningful to a transport and cheap for a fighter to earn. Zero disables the lead test and leaves the old gate.
- **Decision (D) — ONE clock, the one that was already there.** The new test joins the existing escorts-are-up condition inside `LiftMayLaunch` rather than getting a wait of its own: `escortsUp >= escortsWanted && escortsAhead >= escortsWanted && !aradPending`, with the existing `secondsWaiting >= formUpSeconds` short-circuit released ahead of all of it. `CommanderSettings.PackageFormUpSeconds` (180 s) therefore releases the escort wait, the ahead wait and the sweep wait together. A lift can never be stuck, and going unescorted after the wait is today's behaviour, not new behaviour.
- **Decision (E) — the whole escort element has to be in front, not just its leader.** `escortsAhead >= escortsWanted` rather than "at least one ahead". One fighter in front of a transport is not cover; and since an escort that is ahead is by construction an escort that is up, the new count can never exceed the old one and the gate is strictly stronger. Both numbers are kept so the hold line can say which of the two questions is holding the load.
- **Decision (F) — distance is to the LANDING ZONE, and the load's distance is its launch base's.** `order.Point` on a lift order IS the landing zone — for a platoon lift onto contested ground it is the forward landing zone, not the objective beyond it — which is also where the cover sortie is already centred, so there is one point and not two. The load itself does not exist when the gate is asked, so its distance is the distance from the airbase it would lift from, taken from the insertion gate's own `TryFindInsertionLaunchBase` (Reuse rule 4, one definition). That returns the NEAREST held base, so the escort's lead is measured against the shortest leg the load could fly — the strictest reading. With no held base nothing can be measured and the ahead test is not applied at all; a commander with no airbase cannot launch a transport anyway, and a lift must never be held by a comparison that could not be made.
- **Decision (G) — a LAUNCH gate and nothing else.** A transport already in the air is never re-asked. Re-checking in flight would turn a flight round because its cover fell behind, which is not what was asked and would fight the delivery watch that already recalls flights for real threats.
- **Decision (H) — picket insertions are deliberately NOT gated, and it is structural.** A picket flight is refused outright by `TryFindInsertionRouteThreat` when anything that shoots helicopters — any hostile aircraft, or a ground vehicle `IsAirDefence` calls air defence — is tracked within `InsertionThreatRadiusMeters` of the landing zone or the route. That constant IS `ObservedRadiusMeters`, the same ring `AddCapDemand` counts hostile aircraft in when it decides whether a picket point gets a patrol at all. So a picket insertion that launches is by construction a flight to a point that has no cover sortie and can have none: if there were hostile aircraft to open a patrol, the insertion would already have been refused. Its escort, `escort to <point>` from `AddTransportEscortDemand`, is posted only once the record exists and the transport is airborne, as insurance against raiders nobody has tracked. On top of that `InsertionStandoffClear` only flies pickets to rear points more than 25 km from the nearest enemy asset. Gating them would mean holding every picket flight for three minutes waiting on fighters the commander would have to buy, over ground with nothing hostile tracked on it — the regression the brief warned about, with no benefit.
- **Decision (I) — a helicopter escort cannot arise.** `IsFixedWingCombatAircraft` excludes anything with a rotary pilot, so a cover element is fixed-wing by construction and a helicopter is counted neither as up nor as ahead. The new count inherits that test unchanged, so the rule behaves the same way it always has: a gunship flying alongside a lift is invisible to the gate, and the gate waits for the fighters.

- **Root cause 3: even a working restore would have been a frame too late.** `CommanderEnemyCommanderService.nextReviewAt` starts at zero, so the commander's first review runs on its own first tick and opens its treasury there. The store was registered after the commander and waited a further fifteen seconds, so the duel head start and the first spending review both happened before any restore could land. `RTS mode enabled` appearing after the head start in the log is the same observation from the other end.

### DECISION-058: Automatic building placement conforms to the terrain, through the manual path's own rule
- **Date**: 2026-09-18
- **Track**: none — direct user instruction, 2026-09-18: "when spawning fobs etc — or indeed placing any building, it needs to conform to the terrain it is placed on (we use R when manually building) — at the moment we have some partially underground."
- **Problem**: `CommanderEconomyService.SpawnBuilding` composed its rotation as `randomRotation ? Quaternion.Euler(0f, Random.Range(0f, 360f), 0f) : rotation ?? Every automatic placement therefore stood vertical on sloping ground with one corner buried. The manual path had solved this already: `CommanderBuildPreview.PlacementRotation` composed the chosen heading with a sampled ground normal through `CommanderTerrainSlope.Compose`, clamped by `MaxPlacementTiltDegrees`.
- **Decision (A) — one chokepoint, not one fix per call site.** Every building spawn in the mod — the FOB recipe (`CommanderFobBuilder.BuildFobStructures`), the base-facility rung's depot and helipad, mines, factories, naval docks, the enemy commander's radar and defence emplacements, the player's catalogue purchases and the strategic reload's rebuilds — already funnels through the single private `SpawnBuilding`. That is where the tilt is applied, and it is the only place it is applied. `Spawner.SpawnFromUnitDefinitionInEditor` is called elsewhere only by the SAM-site construction and the repair-crew spawn, and neither places a `BuildingDefinition`; the SAM site's own units are vehicles standing on ground its analyser already picked for flatness, and were deliberately left alone.
- **Decision (B) — an explicit rotation means "already composed"; no rotation means "automatic".** A caller that hands in a rotation is the player's click carrying whatever the armed ghost was showing, tilt included, so it is used unchanged and the preview remains exactly what lands. Every other caller gets `CommanderBuildPreview.GroundConformingRotation`. A caller asking for `randomRotation` keeps its random HEADING and gains the tilt: the bug was never the heading.
- **Decision (C) — the same normal the manual path samples, not a better one.** `SampleGroundNormal` is reused verbatim: four `SnapToTerrain` probes west/east/south/north at the building's own footprint radius, floored at `SlopeProbeMinimumRadiusMeters` (12 m), fitted by `CommanderTerrainSlope.EstimateNormal`. Not a single point sample, and not a new sampler — agreeing with the ghost matters more than being cleverer than it, because disagreement is what the player would see.
- **Decision (D) — steeper than the limit CLAMPS; it does not refuse.** `MaxPlacementTiltDegrees` (12 degrees) is reused, not duplicated. Clamping is the manual path's own answer, and its `<summary>` already records why: past about 12 degrees a depot's spawn point and a helipad's landing point start pointing into the hill, and the clamped building looking slightly buried uphill is the honest signal that the ground is too steep. A structure the commander has already committed funds to is better standing at the limit than not standing. No refusal was added at site selection: `CommanderFobBuilder.FobStructureSite` already moves each recipe building to the nearest level spot via `CommanderStrategicPointService.TryFindLevelBuildingSpot`, which is the mod's one levelness rule, and adding a steepness veto to `CanSiteFob` would change where the commander can build — a gameplay change nobody asked for.
- **Decision (E) — no building type is kept upright.** Neither the game nor the manual path treats any type as always-vertical: the conform toggle applies to every definition, and the 12-degree cap exists precisely because helipads and depots are the types that stop working when over-leaned. Inventing a helipad exception here would make an automatically placed pad sit differently from a hand-placed one, which is the drift this change exists to remove.

### DECISION-062: Several attacks at once, each with its own air — and the AB-4 off the patrol
- **Date**: 2026-09-18
- **Track**: `concurrent-attacks_20260918` (design and plan approved by the user; `conductor/tracks/concurrent-attacks_20260918/design.md`). The AB-4 half is a direct user instruction of the same day, taken while the track was executing.
- **Problem**: the developer watched an 81-minute match and reported "endless CAP warfare and not much combined arms". The log said why. One commander's mission board read **48 Picket, 11 ForwardBase, 1 Attack, 1 Reserve** — sixty-one missions, one offensive — with **1 of 10 platoons `Attacking`** and the rest `Moving@ForwardBase` or `Forming`; the other commander 2 of 11. Of 559 aircraft launched, **382 were fighters and 177 ground-attack (68/32)**. The chain: 60 control points generate 59 defensive missions, nearly every platoon is garrison or in transit, one attack crawls forward, the ground generates no close-support work, and the only war left is fighters over points.
- **Root cause, one line**: `Operations/CommanderOperationsOffensive.cs:602-608`. `TryOpenAttack` returned false the moment ANY attack mission existed. One attack per commander, always, whatever the army size.
- **Decision (A) — an allowance shaped like the forward-base one, not a new shape.** `MaxConcurrentAttacks(platoonCount, attacksPerPlatoon, floor)` is written to match `MaxForwardBases` (`Operations/CommanderOperationsFront.cs:2186`) — a floor, plus a share of the platoon count, take the larger (Reuse rule 4). Defaults 2 and 0.2, so the measured ten-platoon commanders get two and a twenty-platoon one gets four.
- **Decision (B) — a non-positive floor answers ONE, not zero.** A deliberate departure from the repo's usual "zero switches the rule off": switching this rule off has to mean the old single attack, because a commander that never attacks is not a commander. Two named checks pin it.
- **Decision (C) — one strike package per open attack, and the package lives on the MISSION.** The single package was a single FIELD, `OperationsState.StrikeSortie`, read in ten places across five files. Rejected alternative: turning it into a list. Two reasons — an attack's package should die with the attack, which a field on the mission gives for free; and the deliberate strike's clock, its one-at-a-time rule and its stand-down path are all built around that slot and are not what this track changes. `state.StrikeSortie` therefore still holds the DELIBERATE strike alone.
- **Decision (D) — one enumerator, and the ten readers all go through it.** `CollectStrikeSorties` yields the deliberate package plus one per attack; `AddStrikeDemand`, `UpdateStrikePackage` and `CloseFinishedStrike` each became a loop over it with their existing bodies moved unchanged into a per-package helper (Reuse rule 3: moved, not rewritten). Without one definition, packages living in two places would have been three chances to forget one.
- **Decision (E) — the forget is SPLIT, and this is the trap the split exists to avoid.** `ForgetStrikeSortie` resets `LastStrikeAt`, `StrikeClockMinutes` and `StrikeClockReported` — the DELIBERATE strike's clock. Had an attack's package closing run that same code, every finished attack would silently have delayed the commander's next deliberate strike. `ForgetAttackStrike` clears an attack's slot and touches no clock; `ForgetAnyStrike` picks the right one, and every abandon path calls it.
- **Decision (F) — `FindStrikeFor` became `mission.StrikeSortie`.** It used to read the single slot and match it back to the mission by point or airbase. With several attacks open that lookup could match the WRONG package when two attacks sat on nearby ground.
- **Decision (G) — an attack may call up a platoon from an unthreatened forward base.** `CountSparePlatoons` counts only platoons with NO mission, which on the measured match was one to three per commander while eight or nine sat on forward bases; without the call-up the raised allowance would have permitted attacks nobody could man — inert. Threat is `IsThreatenedFrontPoint`, the mod's ONE definition of "the enemy is at this point" and already read by the forward-base allowance, the pool order and the order book; this is its fourth reader and invents no second idea of danger. Worst-ranked base first.
- **Decision (H) — the base keeps its picket and its truck.** A picket is what holds ground; stripping one would hand the point over. Only the platoon goes.
- **Decision (I) — the call-up reuses `ReleaseFromMission`,** whose own summary calls it "the single choke point every departure path already goes through". A hand-rolled unassign was written first and thrown away: it would have carried the platoon's ground posture and its reinforcement answer into the attack, which that method exists to clear.
- **Decision (J) — the Alkyon AB-4 is ground-attack only** (user, 2026-09-18: "a large, fast, high-altitude large-payload delivery system ... we might be better just having it as ground-attack only"). Implemented by adding `AirframeTier.Multirole` to `MayFlyAirSuperiority`'s refusal rather than by naming the airframe, because every other rule in that file keys on the game's own role data. **This overturns a deliberate earlier decision** whose named check read `a multirole bomber is still allowed to escort`; that case is now `the Alkyon AB-4 is ground-attack only and flies no fighter task`, expecting false, with the reversal and its date recorded beside it.
- **Decision (K) — and the check that nearly went wrong.** The `AirframeTier` enum's own doc named the **KR-67 Ifrit** as the multirole example. Had that been true, refusing multirole would have grounded Primeva's main fighter — 212 launches in the measured match. The log settles it: the KR-67 is bought at **Fighter** tier every time, and the tier rule is role-independent for non-radar airframes, so the comment was simply stale. It has been corrected and now records the near-miss. The only genuine multirole entries are the AB-4 and the EW-25 Medusa, and the Medusa is already reserved to the radar station.
- **Impact**: `Operations/CommanderOperationsOffensive.cs` (`MaxConcurrentAttacks`, `PlatoonMayBeCalledUp`, `CallUpQuietForwardBases`, the lifted gate, `OpenStrikeSortie`'s owner, `CheckConcurrentAttacks`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsAirPackages.cs` (`CollectStrikeSorties`, `ForgetAttackStrike`, `ForgetAnyStrike`, the two walks split into per-package helpers, `FindStrikeFor`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsAirIdle.cs` (`AddStrikeDemand` split), `Operations/CommanderOperationsAirPosture.cs` (the stand-down), `Operations/CommanderPlatoon.cs` (`CommanderOperationsMission.StrikeSortie`), `Operations/CommanderOperationsService.cs` (the check registration), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAir.cs` (`MayFlyAirSuperiority`, the stale enum comment), `Ai/CommanderEnemyCommanderAirChecks.cs` (the reversed case plus one more), `Core/CommanderSettings.cs` (`MaxAttacks`, `AttacksPerPlatoon`), `UI/CommanderOverlayUiSettings.cs` (two sliders, box height 494 to 570), `CHANGELOG.md`.
